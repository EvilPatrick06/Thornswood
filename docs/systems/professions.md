# Professions

What you make, or how you fight. One profession, then one half of it.
The half is a sub-profession. It is what you can make. A class is a
separate choice and lives under [Classes](classes.md).

The pages below are the professions. Each one links to its
sub-professions, and those pages are where the benches, the rank
counts, and the item list sit.

## The ladder

Four ranks: novice, advanced, expert, master. Everyone starts at novice.

Work is counted in pieces, and the count starts again at each rank:

| Into | Pieces of work | Then |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Advanced | 100 | The count returns to 0 |
| Expert | 250 | The count returns to 0 |
| Master | 600 | There is no further rank |

That is 950 pieces from a new novice to master, not 600 in total.
One successful swing, harvest, or craft is one piece. A master perk
that makes two items from one craft is still one piece of work.

Each rank hands one skill-tree point. See [Skill tree](skill-tree.md).

The same ladder is used by every class. A miner and a tailor are not
on different clocks.

A swing of your own work also costs rest, by the rank you are climbing:

| Rank | Rest |
| --- | --- |
| Novice | 1.5 |
| Advanced | 1.2 |
| Expert | 0.9 |
| Master | 0.6 |

Under 15 rest, crafting is refused. The class verbs still happen, and
they still cost the rest.

Work done while somebody from your party is in the same cell banks
15% of a piece. At a whole piece it pays one extra. That is every
seventh piece. Listed on a party and standing somewhere else pays
nothing. See [People beside you](people.md).

## How a recipe gets its rank

The rank is the dearest ingredient a class produces. An iron cuirass
asks for iron, and iron is novice mining, so the cuirass is novice.
A piece that asks for a steel ingot is advanced, because that is the
rank recorded for gathering what that ingot is made from. An item
has no gold price. What it sells for is the price a person posts.
If nothing in the row is produced by any class, the rank is the one
written on the row when the recipe was added. Each row says that rank.

## What the server allows

Three answers, and each one names somebody.

Your own half, at or under your rank. It is made, and the work counts
on that half.

Your sister half, up to novice. It is made. It does not climb that
half past novice unless a master of that half has you as an apprentice.
An Edgewright can beat out novice plate. A steel cuirass is not novice
plate.

Anything else. It is not made. You keep the materials. The line says
whose work it is and what rank it asks for.

Until a profession is picked, nothing that belongs to a half is made.
Smelting an ingot belongs to the smith halves. Tanning a hide into
leather belongs to the Platewright. One row of each stays. The
duplicate rows are in [Cut items](cut-items.md). House walls, roofs
and furniture are owned by nobody
([lists/anybody.md](lists/anybody.md)), and the Hearthfire building
benches they sit at open for nobody. Nails, hinges, locks and iron
fittings are refused: [lists/refused.md](lists/refused.md).

A recipe can also demand a race. [Races](races.md). The locked rows
are marked in the lists and collected in
[lists/race-locks.md](lists/race-locks.md).

## Apprentices

The rules, the two arrangements, and what a taught novice can and
cannot make are [Masters and apprentices](apprenticeship.md).

A master decides who, and can end it. Nobody is owed one. The sister
half, past novice, needs a master of that half and does not use one
of the two outside trades. An outside trade is a novice list, twice
in a life, and it never climbs.

## Benches, in one picture

A bench opens only for somebody with at least one recipe there that
they may make, so the crafting menu never opens on nothing. The bench
asks the same question the craft does, recipe by recipe, so a rank, a
race's own gear and a roll all count. The menu then lists only what you
may make.

Who each bench opens for. A half is one you can work at the rank named:
your own half, or your sister half or a taught half at novice. No
bench opens for everybody: the few things at a bench that nobody owns
(rings at the forge, ores at the smelter, fur plate at the tanning rack)
are made by whoever the bench opens for.

| Bench | Who |
| --- | --- |
| Oven | Hearthcook or Confectioner, from novice |
| Cookpot | Hearthcook, from novice |
| Grain mill | Hearthcook, from novice |
| Charcoal kiln | Charburner, from novice |
| Alchemy lab | Blightbrewer or Physicker, from novice |
| Arcane enchanter | Bladebinder or Wardweaver, from novice |
| Staff enchanter | Bladebinder, from novice |
| Sharpening wheel | Shut for everybody. Tempering is cut, and a piece is mended with a repair kit |
| Skyforge | Edgewright, Platewright, or Woodwright, at advanced |
| Dawnguard bench | Woodwright, at advanced |
| Forge | Smiths and Tailors, and the Woodwright for bows and arrows, from novice |
| Tanning rack | Platewright or Haberdasher, from novice. Liverywright at advanced |
| Loom | Haberdasher or Liverywright, from novice |
| Armour table | Shut for everybody. Tempering is cut, and a piece is mended with a repair kit |
| Aetherium forge | Shut for everybody. Nobody works it |
| Smelter | Platewright, from novice |

A forge or a skyforge burns one charcoal, cheapest grade in the pack
first. No charcoal, nothing is made. A war axe, a battleaxe, a
warhammer, and a mace also take 1 Tool Handle. Swords, greatswords,
daggers, bows, and staves do not. The handle is novice
[Charburner](professions/woodworker/charburner.md) work, so listing it
does not move the rank. The plugin rows still omit it. The server
already removes one when the craft goes through.

## The book on K

Press K. The recipe book has a column for your half, a column for the
sister half, and a column for each half a master has taught you. It
shows kinds and counts, not every garment. The full item rows are the
lists linked from each sub-profession.

A mage sees the spell book there instead.

## Names that are not a profession

Bard, merchant, priest, scholar and thief are not picked on K. They
grant no recipe, no verb and no rank. Bardic rest is on
[People beside you](people.md). The merchant NPCs belong to the
[Merchant's Guild](merchants-guild.md).

## Staff

Staff have a crafting card on the Office page of K. They pick somebody
in the world and see every class, half and school that person holds, its
rank, and the work counted toward the next. Raise moves one rank, the
way the work would: the same ceilings hold, so a taught trade stays at
novice and a sister half past novice still needs its Master, and the
rank gives its skill point, and a spell point for a mage's school or an
Enchanter's staff spells. The count toward the next rank starts again at
0.

The card also asks the server about a recipe by what it makes: whether
that person may make it, and if not, the reason the bench would give.
When they may make it and the bench still leaves it out, the card says
whether their game was handed an older list than the server gives now,
which a button hands it again, or whether the game's own recipe asks for
a perk, an item or a spell first.

And staff give back an item somebody lost, with a count and a reason.
Nothing records what was lost, so the reason is the record. Nobody does
any of it to themselves, and every change is in the staff log with the
name of the staff member who made it.

## Where this is

The eight professions, the halves, the ladder with the count resetting,
the sister half at novice, the bench table, the charcoal and the
handle, and the recipe lists are in the server. Smelting is a smith's
and tanning is the Platewright's, as above, and neither is one of the
two outside trades. A master takes someone on in the sister half or
in an outside trade from the look prompt, and either of them ends it on
the K panel. See [Masters and apprentices](apprenticeship.md).

The crafting menu at a bench lists only what the craft gate would let
you make there: your profession, your half and its rank, the sister
half at novice, a taught half at novice, your race and your roll. The
server hands your game the rest of the recipe table, and your game takes
those recipes off every bench until the answer changes, so a rank, a
Master taking you on or a roll changes the list straight away. A recipe
nobody owns is listed for everybody.

Not in the world yet: the parts of a warrior's maneuvers the game
has to do itself (see [Warrior](professions/warrior.md)), the repair
kits on [Durability](durability.md), the check of which effect a
brew or an enchantment carries (the engine does not pass it yet),
and the vanilla perk
lines that still hide some recipes in the crafting menu even when the
server would allow them. Each profession
page says which of those is its own.
