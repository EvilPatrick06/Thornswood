The head related impulse responses in the launcher's voice
(launcher\hrtf-composite.wav, built into Thornswood.exe)

They are Chromium's Composite.flac
(third_party/blink/renderer/platform/audio/resources/Composite.flac at
Chromium commit dd2ed7a22c23f9c9a85e9514c8c3308bf6ae4fbe, sha256
40bda2ad9170fa2bcdc2d5f20cdd98c70609ad33a94df49fe7d6007cacab2ada), the same
samples written as 16-bit PCM: 240 stereo responses of 256 frames at
44.1 kHz, the concatenation of Chromium's
IRC_Composite_C_R0195_T<azimuth>_P<elevation>.wav files. Chromium and
Firefox both ship them for Web Audio's HRTF panning.

Where they are from, as Firefox records it
(dom/media/webaudio/blink/IRC_Composite_C_R0195-incl.cpp):

  "The sample data in the arrays here was derived for Webkit by Chris Rogers
  through averaging of impulse responses from the IRCAM Listen HRTF
  Database. The responses here are half the length of the Listen responses.
  This sample data has been granted to the Public Domain."

The IRCAM Listen HRTF Database (http://recherche.ircam.fr/equipes/salles/listen/)
states its terms as follows. IRCAM's page could not be reached on 1 November
2026, so the words are as the Ambisonic Toolkit's licensing notes quote them
(https://depts.washington.edu/dxscdoc/Help/Other/ATKLicensing.html):

  "Copyright (c) 2002 IRCAM (Institut de Recherche et Coordination
  Acoustique/Musique). All Rights Reserved"

  "Use of Materials: The Listen database is public and available for any
  use. We would however appreciate an acknowledgment of the database
  somewhere in the description of your work (e.g. paper) or in your
  development."
