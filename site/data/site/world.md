# Fifty Years of a Free Skyrim

*Being an account of the years since the Crown was settled, set down at
Windhelm in the year 4E 252.*

## The War, and the end of it

The war ended the way wars end, which is badly for everyone and worse for some.

By the close of 4E 202 the Legion held the road and nothing else. Solitude had
been taken in the winter. General Tullius was dead. The Empire, with the
Dominion at its throat and no strength left to spare for a province that had
stopped paying, did the arithmetic and signed.

The terms were short. Skyrim would govern Skyrim. Imperial soldiers would leave
by the first thaw. The Empire kept its claim on paper and nothing else, which
is what a claim is worth without soldiers standing on it.

Ulfric Stormcloak was crowned High King by the Moot at Windhelm in 4E 203. He
was fifty-one. He would hold the crown for twenty-six years.

## The Expulsion

The Thalmor did not leave when the Legion left. They had never been the
Empire's, only its guests, and they saw no reason a change of flag should
trouble them.

In 4E 209 the High King disagreed. What followed is called the Expulsion in the
holds and something less polite everywhere else. The embassy at Haafingar was
emptied in a night. Every Justiciar in Skyrim was given until the new moon to
be over a border, and those who were not were hunted.

The Aldmeri Dominion protested. It did not march. It was still bleeding from a
war of its own and Skyrim, whatever else it was, was no longer worth the cost
of taking.

The Thalmor have been illegal in Skyrim for forty-three years. This has not
stopped them being here.

## The Sundered Years

What followed the Expulsion was not peace. It was the absence of anyone strong
enough to make war.

Justiciars who had not made the border went to ground. Some died in it. Some
did not, and their grandchildren are still here, running gold and poison and
worse under other names, through the holds and along the coast. They call
themselves nothing. Other people call them the Remnant.

Most of them do not want Skyrim back. No Dominion order to retake it has
reached them yet, and no Dominion fleet has yet been seen off the coast. What
they want is what people want when the cause that made them looks forty years
dead: coin, safety, and revenge on whoever is nearest.

They are the most dangerous thing in Skyrim precisely because, as far as
anyone can tell, they answer to nobody any more.

## The White Harbour Accord

The Empire came back the way empires do when they cannot come back with
soldiers. It came back with a ledger.

By 4E 218 the harvests had failed twice, the eastern roads were shut, and
Skyrim was discovering that governing Skyrim included feeding it. Cyrodiil had
grain. Skyrim had ore, timber, and a coastline the Dominion had not yet tried
to blockade.

The talking took three years and one walkout. The Accord was signed at Dawnstar
in 4E 221 and it is the reason there is an Imperial embassy in Solitude today.

The Accord is not an apology and nobody pretends it is. It is a trade in which
both sides got the thing they needed and neither got the thing they wanted. The
Empire wanted the province. It got a warehouse. Skyrim wanted the Empire gone
for good. It got grain.

The full text is kept at the Blue Palace and a copy hangs in every hold's
longhouse, because the High King of the day wanted every Nord able to read
exactly what had and had not been given away.

## The Free Skyrim Trading Company

Article the Third left the Empire one house in Skyrim, and the East Empire
Company was not it. In the spring of 4E 222 its factors at Solitude and
Windhelm sold what they could carry and walked away from what they could not.
The piers had never been theirs to sell. They went back to the holds that stood
over them, as Article the First said every harbour would.

What was left on the docks was a habit: the long, dull business of getting a
crate from one hold to another and being paid for it. The Free Skyrim Trading
Company picked it up. It rents the old warehouses from the Jarls, owns no pier
and no ship, and hires both from whoever has them. It carries for anyone who
pays, keeps its cut when a load arrives, and buys the things that leave Skyrim
for good. The one warehouse the Empire kept is its embassy, and the Company's
are not that one.

Who first put a name to the charter, and whose silver paid for it, is told
three ways in Solitude and a fourth in Windhelm. The signs over the old doors
still say East Empire in places. Paint costs money, and the Company has not yet
decided it is worth spending.

## Now

Ulfric died in 4E 229, in bed, which surprised him as much as anyone.

The Moot sat for eleven days and did not choose his kin. It chose **Eirik
Ravensgard** of the Rift, who had been a housecarl at nineteen and a Jarl's
steward at thirty and who had, crucially, made no enemies worth the name. He
has held the crown for twenty-two years. He is not loved. He is not hated
either, and in Skyrim that is a longer reign.

The crown sits above the holds and below nothing. There is no Emperor over it.
Each hold keeps its own Jarl, its own court, its own law within the High King's
law, and its own opinion of everyone else's, which is the oldest tradition in
the province.

The roads are safer than they were and less safe than they should be. The mines
are running. There is an embassy in Solitude flying a flag that used to fly
over all of this, and its staff are polite to a degree that fools nobody.

Somewhere out past the lantern light there are elves with old orders, and
nobody in Skyrim knows yet whether anyone is left to give them new ones.

Fifty years is not long. Plenty of people remember.

# The White Harbour Accord

*Agreed at Dawnstar, 4E 221, between the Kingdom of Skyrim and the Empire of
Tamriel. In force. Read it before you argue about it.*

This is the whole reason there is an Imperial embassy in a country that threw
the Empire out. It is nine articles. People quote the first and the fourth at
each other in taverns and get both wrong.

---

## Article the First: On the Crown

The Empire of Tamriel recognises the Kingdom of Skyrim as sovereign, entire,
and no part of the Empire.

The High King of Skyrim holds his crown of the Moot and of no other. No oath is
owed to the Ruby Throne by any Jarl, thane, housecarl or free Nord.

The Empire makes no claim, present or future, to any hold, road, mine, or
harbour within Skyrim's borders.

> Everyone reads this one aloud. It is the one Skyrim won.

## Article the Second: On Trade

The ports of Solitude, Dawnstar and Windhelm are open to Imperial shipping on
the same terms as any other.

Skyrim ore, timber, fur, and salt pass into the Empire without tariff.
Cyrodiilic grain, wine, paper and worked goods pass into Skyrim the same.

Neither party shall impose upon the other a duty it does not impose upon
itself.

## Article the Third: On the Embassy

The Empire may maintain one embassy within Skyrim, at Solitude, and no other
house, fort, or station anywhere in the province.

The embassy may hold no more than twenty persons in service, of whom no more
than six may bear arms, and those six within its walls only.

No Legion soldier may set foot in Skyrim under arms. The embassy guard are not
Legion and are not to be called so.

## Article the Fourth: On Law

**This is the article people get wrong, so read it twice.**

An Imperial citizen standing on Skyrim's soil stands under Skyrim's law. The
embassy is not a country. Its walls are a courtesy, not a border. A man who
robs a shop in Solitude and runs through the embassy gate is a man who robbed a
shop in Solitude, and the Ambassador will hand him out or answer for it.

A Nord standing inside the embassy stands under Imperial law, and is owed its
protections in full. This is not a trap and it has been tested. A citizen of
Skyrim who enters those doors to trade, to petition, or to complain cannot be
held there, cannot be questioned without a witness of their own choosing, and
cannot be tried for anything at all.

Neither party may reach into the other and take a person out of it. If the
Empire wants a man, it asks the Jarl. If Skyrim wants a man out of the embassy,
it asks the Ambassador. Both have said no before. Neither has yet made it a
war.

## Article the Fifth: On the Dominion

Both parties hold the Aldmeri Dominion an enemy.

Neither shall harbour, fund, employ, or carry a Justiciar or any agent of the
Dominion, nor conceal one from the other.

Knowledge of Dominion agents shall be shared freely and without price. This
clause has been honoured more faithfully than any other in the Accord, which
tells you what both sides are actually afraid of.

## Article the Sixth: On Talos

No man, woman, or child in Skyrim shall be questioned, fined, watched, or
touched for the worship of Talos.

The Empire acknowledges that this worship is lawful in Skyrim and undertakes
never to say otherwise within its borders.

What the Empire does inside its own borders is the Empire's business and is not
Skyrim's to settle. This sentence took four months of the three years.

## Article the Seventh: On the Roads

Imperial couriers and merchants travel Skyrim's roads under the King's peace,
and are owed the same protection and the same penalty as any Nord.

Skyrim's merchants have the same in Cyrodiil.

Neither party shall arm, pay, or shelter bandits against the other. Both parties
have broken this one and both parties know it.

## Article the Eighth: On Ransom and Return

Prisoners of the war of 4E 201 to 202, living, were returned within one year of
signing. This was done.

The bones of the Imperial dead may be reclaimed by their kin at any time
without hindrance, toll, or question. The bones of Skyrim's dead in Cyrodiil
the same.

## Article the Ninth: On Ending

Either party may end this Accord by declaring so at Dawnstar, in person, before
witnesses of both.

Everything in it stands for one year and one day after such a declaration, so
that merchants may come home, families may cross, and neither side need begin a
war by accident.

---

*Signed by the hand of Ulfric, High King of Skyrim, and by the hand of the
Imperial Legate at Dawnstar, in the two hundred and twenty-first year of the
Fourth Era.*

## What this means if you are playing here

- **You can play an Imperial.** Citizens, merchants, embassy staff, the
  Ambassador's people. You are legal, you are watched, and you are one bad
  night away from being told to leave.
- **You can play a Legion veteran.** Somebody who served forty years ago and
  settled here is a good character to play. You cannot play a serving Legion
  soldier: Article the Third keeps every one of them out of Skyrim under
  arms. The six who bear arms at the embassy are its own guard, not Legion,
  armed inside those walls and nowhere else. Walk out the gate carrying and
  you have broken the Accord, and the Ambassador will hand you over rather
  than answer for you.
- **You can work for the Free Skyrim Trading Company.** It hauls, buys and
  sells between the holds, out of buildings with the wrong name over the door.
  Its lead seat is given rather than chosen, like a Jarl's.
- **You can play a Thalmor.** You are a criminal. There is no embassy to run
  to and no immunity. Whether orders still come from home, and who sends them,
  is yours to play out. Article the Fifth means the Empire will hand you over
  as fast as a Nord will. Play it as what it is: a hunted thing with a long
  memory.
- **You can play a Jarl or a steward.** Those are positions, not characters,
  and they are given rather than chosen. How one comes free, and who decides, is
  something you find out in character.
- **Arguing about the Accord in character is encouraged.** Half of Skyrim
  thinks Article the Fourth gave away too much. The other half has eaten
  Cyrodiilic bread every winter for thirty years.
