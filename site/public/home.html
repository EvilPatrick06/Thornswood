What this is

A world that is not empty at three in the morning

Every other server like this strips the people out completely. The towns go quiet, the shops close, and what is left is a very large field you only enjoy on the nights twenty others happen to be in it. Vanilla Skyrim has the opposite problem: so many faces that none of them matter. Thornswood keeps a chosen few. Enough that a hold feels lived in when two of you are online, few enough that you are still the reason anything happens.

01

Fewer, and worth talking to

Most of the crowd goes. What stays is what a town actually needs: the innkeeper, the smith, the shopkeeper, the guards on the wall. You can buy from them, rob them, and be chased for it.

02

No paperwork once you are in

Getting in is an application while we are playtesting. After that there is no character sheet to have approved, no lore exam, and no waiting on somebody to bless your backstory.

03

The server owns the world

Your character, your gold, your house and every chest in Skyrim live on the server. Nothing is on your machine to lose, and nothing anyone edits on theirs changes what you see.

The World

Fifty years of a free Skyrim

The Stormcloaks won. The Legion marched out in the first thaw of 4E 203 and the Moot put a crown on Ulfric's head. He held it for twenty-six years and died in bed, which surprised him as much as anyone.

The Thalmor were thrown out in 209 and have been illegal ever since. That has not stopped them being here. The Empire came back in 221 the way empires do when they cannot come back with soldiers, and there is an embassy in Solitude flying a flag that used to fly over all of this.

There is a High King of Skyrim and nothing above him. Each hold keeps its own Jarl, its own court, and its own opinion of everyone else's.

The full history and the Accord →

The terms were short. Skyrim would govern Skyrim. Imperial soldiers would leave by the first thaw. The Empire kept its claim on paper and nothing else, which is what a claim is worth without soldiers standing on it. A Free Skyrim, Windhelm, 4E 252

You can play

A Nord of any hold. An Imperial citizen or embassy staff, legal and watched. A Thalmor remnant, hunted by everyone including the Empire. A Jarl, a steward, a housecarl, a guard, a priest, a smith, a thief.

Not the Dragonborn. Not the Listener. Not the last of anything important. Those slots belong to the world.

What you do

A place to live in, not a lobby

All of it server side, so it is the same world for everyone and none of it can be edited away on somebody's own machine.

Voice that carries

Proximity voice in the launcher, not a separate call. Whisper to the person beside you, shout down the length of a hall, and hear the room the way your character would.

Real locks and real keys

A house is locked because there is a lock on it. The key is a thing you carry, hand over, have copied by a smith, or lose to a pickpocket. Anybody can try the lock. Most people should not.

Notice boards in every hold

Pay coin to pin up work, warnings, rumours, a shop opening or a reward for a name. Anyone can read them. Guards can tear them down, and sometimes do.

Nobody can do everything

You pick a profession, one half of it, and a class. An Edgewright cannot make armour. Only a hunter can loot an animal, only a miner can mine. So the leather in your armour came off somebody, and they remember. Professions and classes.

Property worth owning

Go to the hold's steward, pay for the plot, then build it, furnish it and defend it. Land inside a hold means a steward you speak with, not a menu you buy from.

Law with people behind it

Every guard is somebody. Bounties, cells and courts are run by the people holding the keys, so getting caught is not a fine and a loading screen. Somebody has to decide what happens to you.

People with their own work

The ones who stayed are getting jobs, routines and errands of their own, so a quiet hold still has something happening in it and somebody to ask.
