# The White Harbour Accord

*Agreed at Dawnstar, 4E 221, between the Kingdom of Skyrim and the Empire of
Tamriel. In force.*

This is the whole reason there is an Imperial embassy in a country that threw
the Empire out. It is nine articles.

---

## Article the First: On the Crown

The Empire of Tamriel recognises the Kingdom of Skyrim as sovereign, entire,
and no part of the Empire.

The High King of Skyrim holds his crown of the Moot and of no other. No oath is
owed to the Ruby Throne by any Jarl, thane, housecarl or free Nord.

The Empire makes no claim, present or future, to any hold, road, mine, or
harbour within Skyrim's borders.

## Article the Second: On Trade

The ports of Solitude, Dawnstar and Windhelm are open to Imperial shipping on
the same terms as any other.

Skyrim ore, timber, fur, and salt pass into the Empire without tariff.
Cyrodiilic grain, wine, paper and worked goods pass into Skyrim the same.

Neither party shall impose upon the other a duty it does not impose upon
itself.

## Article the Third: On the Embassy

The Empire may maintain one embassy within Skyrim, at Solitude, and no other
house, fort, or station anywhere in the province.

The embassy may hold no more than twenty persons in service, of whom no more
than six may bear arms, and those six within its walls only.

No Legion soldier may set foot in Skyrim under arms. The embassy guard are not
Legion and are not to be called so.

## Article the Fourth: On Law

An Imperial citizen standing on Skyrim's soil stands under Skyrim's law. The
embassy is not a country. Its walls are a courtesy, not a border. A man who
robs a shop in Solitude and runs through the embassy gate is a man who robbed a
shop in Solitude, and the Ambassador will hand him out or answer for it.

A Nord standing inside the embassy stands under Imperial law, and is owed its
protections in full. A citizen of Skyrim who enters those doors to trade, to petition, or to complain cannot be held there, cannot be questioned without a witness of their own choosing, and
cannot be tried for anything at all.

Neither party may reach into the other and take a person out of it. If the
Empire wants a man, it asks the Jarl. If Skyrim wants a man out of the embassy,
it asks the Ambassador.

## Article the Fifth: On the Dominion

Both parties hold the Aldmeri Dominion an enemy.

Neither shall harbour, fund, employ, or carry a Justiciar or any agent of the
Dominion, nor conceal one from the other.

Knowledge of Dominion agents shall be shared freely and without price.

## Article the Sixth: On Talos

No man, woman, or child in Skyrim shall be questioned, fined, watched, or
touched for the worship of Talos.

The Empire acknowledges that this worship is lawful in Skyrim and undertakes
never to say otherwise within its borders.

What the Empire does inside its own borders is the Empire's business and is not
Skyrim's to settle. 

## Article the Seventh: On the Roads

Imperial couriers and merchants travel Skyrim's roads under the King's peace,
and are owed the same protection and the same penalty as any Nord.

Skyrim's merchants have the same in Cyrodiil.

Neither party shall arm, pay, or shelter bandits against the other.

## Article the Eighth: On Ransom and Return

Prisoners of the war of 4E 201 to 202, living, were returned within one year of
signing.

The bones of the Imperial dead may be reclaimed by their kin at any time
without hindrance, toll, or question. The bones of Skyrim's dead in Cyrodiil
the same.

## Article the Ninth: On Ending

Either party may end this Accord by declaring so at Dawnstar, in person, before
witnesses of both.

Everything in it stands for one year and one day after such a declaration, so
that merchants may come home, and families may cross.

---

*Signed by the hand of Ulfric, High King of Skyrim, and by the hand of the
Imperial Legate at Dawnstar, in the two hundred and twenty-first year of the
Fourth Era.*

---

## What this means if you are playing here

- **You can play an Imperial.** Citizens, merchants, embassy staff, the
  Ambassador's people. You are legal, you are watched, and you are one bad
  night away from being told to leave.
- **You cannot play a Legion soldier in Skyrim.** Article the Third. A veteran
  who served forty years ago and lives here now is a different thing.
- **You can work for the Free Skyrim Trading Company.** It hauls, buys and
  sells between the holds, out of buildings with the wrong name over the door.
  Its lead seat is given rather than chosen, like a Jarl's.
- **You can play a Thalmor.** You are a criminal. There is no embassy to run
  to and no immunity. Whether orders still come from home, and who sends them,
  is yours to play out. Article the Fifth means the Empire will hand you over
  as fast as a Nord will. Play it as what it is: a hunted thing with a long
  memory.
- **You can play a Jarl or a steward.** Those are positions, not characters.
  Found out in character.
- **Arguing about the Accord in character is encouraged.** Half of Skyrim
  thinks Article the Fourth gave away too much. The other half has eaten
  Cyrodiilic bread every winter for thirty years.
