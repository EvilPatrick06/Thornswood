# Rules in the World

The Discord rules are about being decent to each other. These are about playing
here. They are different jobs, so they are different lists.

Thornswood is looser than the big roleplay servers on purpose. You do not need
a character sheet approved by three people to walk into a tavern. What you do
need is to not ruin somebody else's evening.

---

## 1. Your character knows what your character knows

You read in Discord that somebody got robbed on the Whiterun road. Your
character did not. Acting on it is metagaming and it is the fastest way to make
a world feel fake.

This cuts both ways and the good half is better: **ask**. "Has word got round
about the road?" is a fun question. Somebody will tell you in character and now
it is real.

## 2. You do not decide what happens to other people

You swing. They decide whether it lands.

Write what you attempt, not what you achieve. `/me swings the axe at his
shoulder` leaves them a choice. `/me buries the axe in his shoulder` takes it
away.

Same with catching, grabbing, robbing, poisoning, convincing and anything else
done to somebody who has their own hands on a keyboard.

## 3. Nobody dies without agreeing to

Your character can be beaten, robbed, broken, humiliated and left in a ditch,
and none of that needs permission beyond rule 2.

**Death does.** If a scene is heading somewhere final, say so out of character
and make sure everyone wants it. A death somebody agreed to is the best thing
that happens on a server like this. A death somebody did not is how you lose a
person for good.

If you go down in the world and nobody agreed to anything, you woke up in a
temple. That happened. Move on.

## 4. Fights between people are agreed first

Combat against the world is what the game is for. Go and get killed by a
draugr.

Combat against another character needs both of you wanting it. One line out of
character before you draw is enough: "up for a scrap?" Nobody has ever been
annoyed by being asked.

Ambushing somebody is a great scene when they signed up for the possibility
and a bad one when they were mid-conversation about cheese.

## 5. Theft and property

You can pick a lock. You can lift a key. You can talk your way in.

You cannot empty someone's house because you found it unlocked while they were
offline. Take something. Take a good thing. Leave the rest, leave a trace, and
make it a story they get to have when they log in, rather than a spreadsheet
they have to rebuild.

If you were robbed and it went too far, say so. It gets put back. Nobody is
going to defend a bad night out.

## 6. Play a person, not a legend

You can be extraordinary. You cannot be the Dragonborn, the Listener, the
Archmage, the Harbinger, or the last of anything important.

Those slots are either taken by the world or they are nobody's. A very good
smith with a bad temper will get you more scenes in a month than a demigod will
in a year, because people can stand next to a smith.

## 7. Titles are given, or taken properly

Jarl, steward, housecarl, thane, captain, priest of a named temple.

Ask for an empty one and it is probably yours. Most of them are open.

Want one somebody already holds? Take it. Scheme, challenge them, put a knife
somewhere useful, turn their court against them. That is the best story on the
server and nobody is going to stop you having it.

Take it **in the world, with them in it**, though. Announcing that you are the
Jarl of Riften while the Jarl of Riften is asleep is not taking a title, it is
editing your own name. Rules 2 and 3 still apply: you swing, they decide
whether it lands, and nobody dies without agreeing to.

Staff roles are not in this. Moderator and Gamemaster are jobs, not titles, and
you cannot depose one in character.

## 8. Use `/ooc` in the world

There is a command for it. `/ooc brb, dog`.

It reaches whoever is standing near you and nobody else, and it comes out in
brackets with your name on it, so there is never a question about whether your
character said it.

Nobody minds a quick one. Everybody minds a fight scene with three lines about
Discord notifications in the middle of it, so keep the long ones for the out of
character channels.

## 9. If it would not be fun to be on the other end of it, do not

This is the rule the other eight are made of.

Slavery, torture as a hobby, sexual content, tormenting a character whose owner
has gone quiet, following somebody who is clearly trying to leave. If you are
about to do a thing and the other person has not indicated they want it, they
do not want it.

## 10. Break character to fix things

If a scene is going somewhere you do not like, stop it. `/ooc can we not` ends
anything, immediately, with no explanation owed and no argument permitted.

Anybody who ignores that is done here. That is the one rule with no second
chance.

---

## What we are not going to be strict about

Because the point of this server is that it should be worth logging into.

- **Accents, dialect and prose.** Write how you like. Nobody is grading you.
- **Lore trivia.** If you have your Second Era dynasties slightly wrong, so
  does Bethesda.
- **Being in character every second.** You can stand in a field and mess about.
- **Your character sheet.** There is not one. Introduce yourself in
  `#character-introductions` in a paragraph and that is enough.
- **Being available.** Nobody owes anybody scheduled roleplay.
- **Playing a race, faction or profession.** Anyone can play anything, except
  the things rules 6 and 7 cover.

## How this is enforced

Somebody talks to you first. Almost every problem on a server this size is a
misunderstanding and ends there.

After that, a Gamemaster gets involved. After that, a Moderator.

Rules 9 and 10 skip to the end.
